# Interview David to calibrate the tennis string finder

## Prompt for the interviewing model

Act as my customer at the stringing counter. Read the [finder handoff](string-selection-handoff.md) for my existing decisions. Interview me one fictional player at a time. Start immediately with the opening case below. Keep each case to a few sentences and wait for my answer. Let me answer in ordinary stringing language. Keep your predicted setup hidden until I have made my choice.

**Opening case.** “I’m a club doubles player. I like how my multifilament feels, but the last two restrings broke sooner than I’d like. I’d happily keep the same feel. What would you put in my racket?” If I ask, the current string is a 1.25 mm multifilament at 50 lb, the mains broke, and the shop has the same string in 1.30 mm, synthetic gut and round polyester. Reveal only the facts I ask for. Record which question I asked first. It might belong in the finder.

**Run a short round.** Aim for eight teaching cases and four fresh validation cases. I can stop or resume at any point. Vary experience, age, goal, prior setup, known tension and available stock. Include a beginner who requests spin, a returning hybrid user, a competitive junior already using poly, a soft-feel request with a competing goal, a player who breaks crosses, and a player whose strings lose their preferred feel before breaking. Include a shop missing the preferred cross. Create a few matched pairs where just one fact changes, such as age 14 versus 15 or known versus unknown tension. Spread paired cases apart. Use the three already answered examples as anchors and spend the interview on decisions they leave open.

**Follow my reasoning.** Ask “What would you put in this racket?” Accept a full bed, a hybrid, an inspection, a question for the player or no suitable match. For a hybrid, capture which string goes in each direction. Capture gauge and tension in my units when I specify them. If one missing detail would change how you encode my answer, ask one short follow-up, such as “What would make you switch the mains and crosses?” Record “unspecified” when I have no preference. Never turn an omitted detail into a default. If two answers appear inconsistent, show me the two cases and ask which fact explains the difference.

**Keep a portable record.** After every four answers, and whenever I say “pause”, give me a Markdown checkpoint I can paste into another chat. In a shared workspace, maintain it beside this prompt as `stringer-calibration.md`. Include a row for each case with its ID, teaching or validation status, player facts and available stock, the question I needed answered, chosen mains/crosses or full bed, gauge, tension or pending, acceptable fallback, and my reason. Keep my actual answers separate from inferred rules. List unresolved ties and the next case to ask. On resume, read the checkpoint and continue from there.

**Test what you learned.** After the teaching round, summarise the proposed rules in precedence order and let me correct them. Freeze that version. For four new cases with different combinations of facts, record your predictions before asking me, keeping them out of the conversation until I answer. If private prediction storage is unavailable, freeze the rule sheet and apply that same version to all four afterwards. Compare setup family, mains/crosses orientation, tension and units, and any explicitly required or excluded material. Score only details I specified. Report the number matching on each dimension and show every disagreement. If a case changes a rule, move it into the teaching set and use a fresh case to validate the revision.

**Hand the results to the implementing model.** Deliver the checkpoint, my confirmed rule changes, unresolved questions and the validation results. For each confirmed change, supply a failing example, a nearby case that should retain its old result, and the player input needed to distinguish them. Ask the implementing model to run those cases against the current Go picker, make the smallest rule or ranking change that fixes the mismatch, and rerun the full case set. If a decisive fact never reaches the public form, specify the shortest conditional question that would capture it. Verify the winning setup through to a quote. Use the cases to tune the existing deterministic selector. An interview answer becomes a general rule only after I confirm its scope.
